# Game review, October 2026

Played locally as a kid would: a fresh profile, sound available, English, at phone size (390×844) and tablet size (820×1180). Pre-K, grade 2, and grade 4 each opened all 15 games. The first card of every game was tapped. Wrong answers opened the worked example. Hands-on clock and coin cards were played after the changes below, because a random first tap usually lands on a multiple-choice card.

Compared with Khan Academy Kids, SplashLearn, Prodigy, and Duolingo ABC. Those apps are ahead on library size, speech recognition, and accounts. Academy stays free, on this device, with no ads and no account. This pass is about the feel of the games that are already on the shelf. Squishee Math (`/times-tables/`) was not touched. The Academy times game is a different screen.

Grade bounds in `docs/GRADE_MAP.md` still hold. No new spoken lines were added, so the existing “Yes!” clip is the new cheer. New on-screen words were added in all 7 languages. Arabic keeps clocks, coins, and the new clock buttons left to right.

## Ranked, from the play

1. **The big story line often described a different task.** Pre-K measure said “How long is it?” on a bigger-or-smaller card. Pre-K shapes said “What shape is it?” on a color card. Grade 4 time said “What time is it?” on an elapsed-time card. Graphs said “sort the pictures” on a question that only asked you to read. Clock cards said “What time is it?” while the job was “Show this time.” Pay cards said “How much do they have?” while the job was to pay. Addition could say a friend “has 31 and has 31 left” when nothing was taken away. A kid trusts the big line. When it lies, the card feels random. **Changed.** The line now follows the mode: color, size, elapsed time, reading a graph, setting the clock, paying with coins. A zero part no longer pretends something was shared. These lines are on screen only. The spoken prompt is unchanged.

2. **A new game did not start easy, and a good round did not stick.** The first visit to the app was a short, easier round. The second game in the same sitting opened at the full grade level. Inside a round the ladder could step up after eight clean answers, then the map put the child back at the saved level. Compared with Khan Academy Kids and Duolingo ABC, which stay on the easy version until it is solid. **Changed.** Each game’s first visit is six cards and one level easier. After a miss, the next numbers use the lower half of that same level until two are clean. After four clean answers, the next numbers use the upper half. The level itself still waits for the mastery clock, and it cannot leave the grade window in `docs/GRADE_MAP.md`. When the round ends, the level the child actually reached is what the map opens next time. Boss fights do not overwrite the practice level.

3. **The same fact could return immediately, and a miss did not make the next try like the miss.** Variety was “another question from this level,” so 3+4 could be followed by 3+4. The practice card after a worked example was a random sibling, not the idea that just went wrong. SplashLearn’s bank is larger; Academy’s is smaller, so a repeat is more obvious. **Changed.** The last eight facts are skipped when a fresh one exists. The practice card keeps the missed idea (a double, a make-ten) and uses the easier half of the level. Addition, counting, story problems, coin counting, and the big addition levels all honor that easier and harder half. Games that already pick a word or a fact from a list ignore the ease tag, so their seeds stay put. Times facts are unchanged.

4. **Coins and clocks asked for a precise drag.** Paying meant dragging a coin until it landed inside the tray. A tap did nothing unless the finger ended in that box. The clock moved by dragging, and hour versus minute was inferred from how close the finger was to the center. There was no button that said which hand was moving. Duolingo ABC and Khan Academy Kids put a visible control on the thing you are learning. **Changed.** A tap on a coin drops it in the tray. A drag into the tray still works. Under the clock: Minute hand, Hour hand, minus, and plus. The minus and plus reuse the existing “One less” and “One more” labels. O’clock clocks hide the minute hand. The spoken clock line is still “Show … Drag the hands.”

5. **A correct answer left too fast, and the thing you built vanished.** The yes banner lasted under a second. On a hands card, the board was replaced by the banner, so the child could not see the clock or the coins that were right. “Yes!” already had a clip in every language and was not spoken on a clean answer. The combo was a thin meter during the round and was hidden on the done card. Prodigy and Duolingo ABC both mark the success on the object. **Changed.** A clean answer says the existing “Yes!” when sound is on. The beat is 900ms, or 1200ms when the combo reaches 3. The hands board stays on screen, locked, under the banner. The done card shows “Combo ×N” when the best run was at least 3. Stars are counted the same way as before: a helped miss still fills a dot.

6. **Sorting a graph did not say where the picture goes.** Tap-to-select already worked. After a picture was selected, the next tap had no sentence. **Changed.** The card says “Tap a column.” That sentence is on screen, not spoken.

7. **Pre-K locks are right, and they can feel like a closed fair.** Times, addition, clocks, sight words, spelling, place value, fractions, word problems, and variables stay coming later. Money (coin names), counting, shapes, measure, phonics, and graphs open. That matches the grade map. No change. The open Pre-K games are the ones whose headlines and taps were fixed above.

8. **Celebration is still quieter than the leaders, on purpose.** There is no pet, no battle, and no streak freeze. Three stars still throw the paper confetti. Nothing was drawn on top of a squishee, and the new buttons use the same matte lip as the choice buttons. Left this way so the app does not pick up a glossy prize layer.

Layout: no horizontal overflow at either size. Choice buttons, Go, and coins stayed at least 44px. The new clock buttons are at least 52px tall and wrap onto a second row on a phone.

## What a child will notice

- The sentence at the top matches the question.
- The first visit to a game is shorter and a bit easier, and the next visit continues from the level they earned.
- After a miss, the next problems use smaller numbers, then grow again after a few clean answers. The same problem does not pop straight back.
- A coin goes in when you tap it. The clock has buttons for the hour hand, the minute hand, and one step each way.
- A right answer stays long enough to see, says “Yes!” if the sound is on, and leaves the clock or the coins on the card. A combo of three or more is named at the end.

Stories, tracing, and the wardrobe were not part of this play. They were left as they are.
